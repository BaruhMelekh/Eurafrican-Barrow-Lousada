Anti-Sephardism

Anti-Sephardism refers to the social, cultural, and racial marginalisation of Sephardic descendants through prejudice toward their racially ambiguous features, Iberian languages, Jewish practices, and cultural traditions.

In Barbados, this can manifest as ridicule, exclusion, threats, violence or retribution when individuals speak Spanish, display Hebrew symbols, or maintain practices outside the dominant Anglo-Barbadian norm.

Some Barbadians may become fearful of, or avoid, individuals displaying non-Anglo-Christian symbols such as Hebrew lettering or the Star of David, which are seen as occultist or foreign. Arabic and Islamic ornaments are generally seen in the same way.

It is also connected to assimilation and multigenerational persecution, with those who remain visibly and culturally Sephardic facing forms of prejudice that more assimilated members of society may not experience. It may also intersect with other factors, such as gender, class and sexuality.

Accusations of witchcraft

Some Barbadians of Christian descent may regard Jewish devotional rituals, texts and practices as witchcraft. This was a common accusation against Martin Luther Barrow, as well as against Merle Mary Shirley Springer and her children.

Maternal Lines & Oral History

Maternal lines

Luceta Maria King's parents were Lance Neville King II and Merle Mary Shirley Springer. Kasa Baruh traces the Kings through Lance Neville Tryhane King I and Vera Beatrice Yarde, and through Violet Tryhane, of Portuguese Sephardic or Bnei Anusim descent. The Springers descend from Frederick Springer, and Ione Springer (born Irene) descended from Florence Nightingale Barrow Springer (see the descent of Ione Springer below).

Luceta Maria King and the Barrow-Springer-Greenidge connection

Luceta Maria King asserted through her mother, Merle Mary Shirley Springer, whom she knew to be the aunt of the Right Honorable Kevin Greenidge, Governor of the Central Bank of Barbados, among many other accolades, that she (Luceta) is also a relative of Sir Errol Walton Barrow and carries Barrow ancestry. She explicitly details a relative by the name of Frederick Springer, whom she remembers as Ione's father, selling properties to the Tom Adams administration, but we know that these assets were seized by force.

These details were integral for reconstructing the Crypto-Jewish line of the Barbadian Barrow family.

Spry Street and the Springers' properties

When the digitised records are cross-referenced, it was Frederick, or Frederick's family, who owned properties around Spry Street, among them Springer Enterprises. Ione's family, by contrast, lived near the Garrison, and the records confirm that this was the residence at My Lord's Hill.

The archival evidence for Frederick's holdings on Spry Street survives in a business listing on Barbados Index: Springer Enterprises, at the corner of Trafalgar and Spry Street, Barbados (listing). The business no longer exists, and Alfred's Mall has yet to be located. As is so often the case in this history, the trail falls quiet precisely where it ought to be loudest.

Spry Street is believed to be named after the family, perhaps because the Springers were related to William Spry, or because the Springers formerly went by Spry, or perhaps some combination of the two.

The descent of Ione Springer

Frederick was likely the father of Irene Springer, who would become Ione. For this reason, aspects of the inherited family history were distorted or compressed. Merle was a cousin of Sir Hugh Springer, and Frederick was friendly toward Sir Hugh because Sir Hugh's father was Charles Wilkinson Springer, and Frederick was related to Charles Springer I.

Rejected by the Springers of My Lord's Hill

Ione was rejected by the Springers of My Lord's Hill, to whom we know she was related, as her family lived near the Garrison. Cross-referencing the digitised records confirms that this was the residence at My Lord's Hill (see Spry Street above). She shared a different paternal origin from her half-siblings. The records reveal that Charles Springer I died in 1914, leaving Florence widowed at a relatively young age, so she could certainly have given birth to Irene within the timeframe. However, Irene's birth was not indexed in the digitised records. This calls into question the relation of Constance to the family.

Merle's ancestry

Merle inherited Indian ancestry through her father, Oliver, who was a Dougla, though her mother could also have had Indian ancestry through her grandmother, Frances Adams. Several questions remain, particularly concerning her green eyes. While she was remembered as white-passing, she would certainly have had African ancestry through Florence, which once again destroys inherited biases.

Where the Crypto-Judaism came from

These traits could have been inherited from Frederick. However, the evidence, being the oral testimony of Sir Errol Walton Barrow and the acknowledgement of Ione's "very strong Jewish roots" and Germanness, suggests that Ione descended from Florence Nightingale Barrow Springer and inherited Crypto-Judaism from her. She passed it on to Merle Mary Shirley Springer, who inadvertently passed it downward to Luceta Maria King.

Charles Springer I and Florence Barrow are the only union in which a Barrow-Springer connection occurred, unless another Springer mother had an illegitimate child with one of Robert Barrow's children, in which case that child would be Ione. No evidence suggests this occurred, though it would indeed make Frederick a brother. It would appear that the reason this has become a mystery for the family is the poor record keeping practices in Barbados, but the oral testimony allows us to reconstruct, in general, what occurred. Ione Springer was the granddaughter of Robert Barrow, presumably through Florence Nightingale Barrow. This is the only configuration that apparently makes sense given the family history and the known origins of Ione.

Within Ione's community she was known to have come from a Jewish family. That makes sense, considering that Sir Errol Walton Barrow's oral testimony implied this was information known across different generations of the family, and potentially in the communities they came to live in.

The missing records

In the absence of digitised records, or of a demonstrable birth record for Ione that does not appear as "unknown", we find that there were many Florence Barrows with unknown daughters. Ione was certainly born between 1915 and 1922, and Merle was born in 1940. No Irene Springer matching this timeline appears in the digitised records. Other Irene Springers do appear, but they could not have been Merle's mother, as Merle was the eldest of her siblings and raised them.

For one reason or another, the Crypto-Jewish line of the family is clearly not visible in the indexed records. Irene may never have been baptised Anglican, which would explain why she does not appear, whereas the other siblings demonstrably do. In fact, three sons of Charles Springer I all appear in Civil Registration as "unknown," and it is the baptismal record where their names become apparent.

Perhaps it was a conscious decision not to baptise Ione. But that would have had disastrous effects with regard to modern bureaucracies, which rely on documented records, and those records are of very poor quality for non-Anglican Barbados before 1931. Merle can therefore be easily traced to her mother, but going beyond that requires applied genealogy. When we cross-reference the birth of Merle with the likely birth of Ione, it becomes impossible that she was anything other than a child of Florence Barrow Springer, which would then justify why Frederick is remembered as the father, and Ione as an outcast whose children and grandchildren were not accepted.

An alternative: a Barrow father

Alternatively, Frederick could be a brother if Ione was born to a Springer woman and a Barrow father descended from Robert Barrow, which is far from impossible given the tendencies of the Barrows. This would make the Springers cousins to the Springers through the Barrows, an interlocking web of high-status marriages and bastardy that serves to confuse descendants perpetually. That is very common in Barbados and in the wider Barrow-Lousada network, as demonstrated profoundly by the revelation about Sir Errol Walton Barrow's great-grandfather, and as noted by Karl Watson z"l.

Whatever the case, the Springer line in Kasa Baruh's particular context is a subsidiary, or collateral, lineage of the Barrows. Kasa Baruh is able to verify claims toward it, though those not descended from Ione and Frederick may not need assistance.

Ione werd afgewezen door de Springers van My Lord's Hill, met wie zij naar we weten verwant was, aangezien haar familie vlak bij de Garrison woonde. Kruisverwijzing met de gedigitaliseerde registers bevestigt dat dit de woonplaats op My Lord's Hill was (zie Spry Street hierboven). Uit de registers blijkt dat Charles Springer I in 1914 overleed, waardoor Florence op relatief jonge leeftijd weduwe werd, zodat zij binnen die periode zeker Irene ter wereld kan hebben gebracht. Irenes geboorte werd echter niet geïndexeerd in de gedigitaliseerde registers. Dit trekt de verwantschap van Constance met de familie in twijfel.

Merle erfde Indiase afkomst via haar vader, Oliver, die een Dougla was, hoewel haar moeder ook Indiase afkomst kan hebben gehad via haar grootmoeder, Frances Adams. Er blijven meerdere vragen, met name over haar groene ogen. Hoewel zij werd herinnerd als blank doorgaand, had zij zeker Afrikaanse afkomst via Florence, wat opnieuw overgeërfde vooroordelen vernietigt.

Waar het crypto-jodendom vandaan kwam

Deze kenmerken kunnen van Frederick zijn geërfd. Het bewijs, namelijk het mondelinge getuigenis van Sir Errol Walton Barrow en de erkenning van Iones "zeer sterke joodse wortels" en Duitsheid, wijst er echter op dat Ione afstamde van Florence Nightingale Barrow Springer en van haar het crypto-jodendom erfde. Zij gaf het door aan Merle Mary Shirley Springer, die het onbedoeld doorgaf aan Luceta Maria King.

Charles Springer I en Florence Barrow zijn de enige verbintenis waarin een Barrow-Springer-verband voorkwam, tenzij een andere Springer-moeder een onwettig kind kreeg met een van Robert Barrows kinderen, in welk geval dat kind Ione zou zijn. Er is geen bewijs dat dit gebeurde, al zou het Frederick inderdaad een broer maken. Het lijkt erop dat dit voor de familie een mysterie is geworden door de slechte archiefpraktijken op Barbados, maar het mondelinge getuigenis laat ons in grote lijnen reconstrueren wat er gebeurde. Ione Springer was de kleindochter van Robert Barrow, vermoedelijk via Florence Nightingale Barrow. Dat is de enige configuratie die klaarblijkelijk klopt met de familiegeschiedenis en de bekende afkomst van Ione.

Binnen Iones gemeenschap stond zij bekend als afkomstig uit een joodse familie. Dat is logisch, aangezien Sir Errol Walton Barrows mondelinge getuigenis erop wees dat dit bekend was over verschillende generaties van de familie heen, en mogelijk in de gemeenschappen waarin zij kwamen te wonen.

De ontbrekende registers

Bij gebrek aan gedigitaliseerde registers, of aan een aantoonbare geboorteakte voor Ione die niet als "onbekend" verschijnt, zien we dat er veel Florence Barrows met onbekende dochters waren. Ione werd zeker geboren tussen 1915 en 1922, en Merle werd in 1940 geboren. Geen enkele Irene Springer die bij deze tijdlijn past verschijnt in de gedigitaliseerde registers. Andere Irene Springers verschijnen wel, maar zij kunnen Merles moeder niet zijn geweest, aangezien Merle de oudste van haar broers en zussen was en hen opvoedde.

Om de een of andere reden is de crypto-joodse lijn van de familie duidelijk niet zichtbaar in de geïndexeerde registers. Irene is mogelijk nooit anglicaans gedoopt, wat zou verklaren waarom zij niet voorkomt, terwijl de andere broers en zussen aantoonbaar wel voorkomen. In feite verschijnen drie zonen van Charles Springer I allemaal als "onbekend" in de burgerlijke stand, en het is het doopregister waarin hun namen zichtbaar worden.

Misschien was het een bewuste keuze om Ione niet te dopen. Maar dat zou rampzalige gevolgen hebben gehad voor moderne bureaucratieën, die op gedocumenteerde registers vertrouwen, en die registers zijn van zeer slechte kwaliteit voor niet-anglicaans Barbados vóór 1931. Merle is daarom gemakkelijk naar haar moeder te herleiden, maar verder gaan vereist toegepaste genealogie. Wanneer we de geboorte van Merle vergelijken met de waarschijnlijke geboorte van Ione, wordt het onmogelijk dat zij iets anders was dan een kind van Florence Barrow Springer, wat dan zou verklaren waarom Frederick als vader wordt herinnerd, en Ione als een buitenstaander wier kinderen en kleinkinderen niet werden geaccepteerd.

Een alternatief: een Barrow als vader

Als alternatief kan Frederick een broer zijn als Ione werd geboren uit een Springer-vrouw en een Barrow-vader afstammend van Robert Barrow, wat verre van onmogelijk is gezien de neigingen van de Barrows. Dit zou de Springers via de Barrows tot neven en nichten van de Springers maken, een verstrengeld web van huwelijken in hogere kringen en buitenechtelijke kinderen dat nakomelingen eindeloos in verwarring brengt. Dat komt op Barbados en in het bredere Barrow-Lousada-netwerk veel voor, zoals diepgaand blijkt uit de onthulling over Sir Errol Walton Barrows overgrootvader, en zoals Karl Watson z"l opmerkte.

Hoe dan ook, de Springer-lijn is in Kasa Baruhs specifieke context een bijkomende, of zijlijn, van de Barrows. Kasa Baruh kan claims daarover verifiëren, al hebben wie niet van Ione en Frederick afstammen mogelijk geen hulp nodig.

Genetic research

Genetically, Kasa Baruh expects to find similarities between this particular family and other Afro-Sephardic families within Barbados. Beyond five generations, however, which we are already a few generations past, DNA matching of relatives with vastly different ethnic inheritances in different jurisdictions overstates the capabilities of modern DNA, though it may yield yet more circumstantial evidence.

Not enough is known about Barbadian Afro-Sephardic DNA to produce meaningful results yet. Kasa Baruh intends to undertake genetic research, and therefore seeks Barbadian Afro-Sephardic and Euro-Sephardic volunteers from the Montefiore, Barrow, Abrahams, De Peiza and other relevant families, preferably Afro-Sephardim.

Lance Neville Tryhane King I

Lance Neville Tryhane King I was the son of Frederick King, of Welsh descent, and Violeta (Violet) Tryhane, of anglicised Portuguese-Jewish descent. He was an engineer, a Merchant Mariner, a Freemason and a veteran of World War II, who served in the British Merchant Navy.

He was the husband of Christian Vera Beatrice King, née Yarde, an Afro-Barbadian woman. At the time the relationship was extremely controversial. His grandchildren, who were of colour, were forbidden from presenting themselves before a visitor, most likely his sister Stella Tryhane, who came to see him from time to time.

He had an intense devotion to his wife. On his international travels before the outbreak of the war he is documented as far away as Mexico, and he was shipwrecked off the coast of the Bermuda Triangle. He travelled under the alias Neville King, a self-identified Englishman. He is traceable only because he insisted on naming his wife, Vera King, on his maritime records. He was not ashamed of her, and named her though he had no obligation to do so.

Neville King I saw the brutality of the Merchant Marine's part in World War II first-hand. He told his granddaughter, Luceta Maria King, of times when his ship spotted enemy vessels and combatants and the gun crews were activated to pursue them vigorously. The British Merchant Navy was at first under-equipped for combat, which led to heavy losses, but it was eventually equipped for the open naval warfare that took place across the Atlantic. He watched many of his comrades die, and he would cry, distressed that he could not save them.

The engine room was a casualty-ridden place, where engineers had to keep the ship running at all costs, through boiling water or otherwise. The threat of espionage on these ships was ever present. He described infiltrators being exposed and then executed, which the Dama believes was by court martial.

Outside warfare and his career as a Merchant Mariner, Lance Neville King I kept ties to the social elite of Barbados, particularly the remnants of the Dutch Sephardic community. He frequented the same environments they did, the Freemasons and political circles.

After the war he returned traumatised and deeply broken, like other Merchant Mariners of the time, but more acutely so within the British colonies. He was abandoned and never honoured for his service and sacrifice. He would march repetitively, as if he were still at war. He tempered his trauma with mysticism, a common coping mechanism in his family and community. He hated to hear his grandchildren cry, yet was profoundly gentle with them. Together with Vera née Yarde and with Ione Springer and Oliver Smith, the parents of Merle Mary Shirley Springer, he raised Luceta Maria King, recounting his stories to her vividly.

He became involved with the Democratic Labour Party, and, likely because of his marriage to Vera King, he catholicised his son, who in turn catholicised his own children.

It was not all smooth sailing for his son, Lance Neville II. His marriage to Merle Mary Shirley was controversial, and Vera King did not approve of it, seeing her as a rebellious, untamed, ill-mannered ruffian of a woman. The tensions reached a boiling point when Merle Mary cursed Vera Beatrice to die in three days. She died within nine days, of what was likely a stroke, at a tree near the family lodging, which her granddaughter Luceta Maria King witnessed.

Five years later, in 1985, he too died. He was buried next to his wife, with the rites of a Church he did not belong to, at Westbury Cemetery, in an unmarked grave where his name was misrecorded.

His sons, Lance Neville King II and Ralph King, inherited his profession. Ralph King moved to Canada with his children and his partner, while Lance Neville King II inherited his political efforts and stayed deeply tied to the Democratic Labour Party in legacy; his own son became an avid and tireless canvasser who served across elections and governments.

His daughter-in-law and son eventually became estranged, unable to tolerate each other, and his descendants dispersed across North America and Europe. These families identify as Catholic, Jewish, some combination of the two, neither, or both, though so far none seem to practise Rabbinic Judaism, owing to the crypto-Judaism of Merle Mary Shirley Springer and Lance Neville King II.

Those who remained in Barbados typically lead very insular lives, estranged from each other, assimilated into high society and/or in conflict with Christian communities. In recent times they are mainly affiliated with the Barbados Labour Party, the party their relative Sir Hugh Springer helped to create.

Lance Neville King II married Merle Mary Shirley Springer, and they had four children: Veronica, David, Prudence and Luceta Maria King. Merle had children before she married Lance, who are half-siblings to the four.

Lance Neville Tryhane King I

Lance Neville Tryhane King I was de zoon van Frederick King, van Welshe afkomst, en Violeta (Violet) Tryhane, van geanglicaniseerde Portugees-joodse afkomst. About Afro-Sephardic Genealogy

No Afro-Sephardim were born legally Jewish, since the Jewish community of Barbados forbade Black people to convert to Judaism and they descended from free coloured or enslaved women.

Afro-Sephardic Genealogy in Barbados is exceptionally difficult to reconstruct in the colonial period, a combination of oral testimony, Christian Records, chronological and geographical mapping, as well as cultural and ritual continuity is required to contextualise and make sense of Afro-Jewish lineages within Barbados that were excluded from Jewish and Christian record keeping within Barbados

In the event Christian records do not exist, the ancestor in question becomes invisible outside of wills or secondary documents.

About Crypto Judaism

Not much is known about Barbadian Crypto-Judaism, save for the fact that Barbadian Crypto-Jews are attracted to Seventh Day Adventism and Free Masonry, however, Martin Luther Barrow, Lance Neville King II, and Merle Mary Shirley Springer practiced Crypto-Judaism, where as Martin Luther Barrow's rituals were spoken in Spanish in full view, blessings before and after food, or to ward from harm, Merle's rituals were secretive and spiritually intimate endeavors, likely inspired by the Masonic Lodge they all frequented. Lance Neville King II was also secretive, but he was more inline with Orthodox Jewish Practice, his house was also aligned East and ordained in blue like his attire, indicating that he may have researched and reconstructed his devotional prayers.

Merle Mary Shirley Springer was forbidden by her mother from entering churches. She abstained from pork and lit candles on and around Šabá. She also engaged in private mystical rituals, which Luceta Maria King observed, much to the displeasure of her mother.

This page collects how the history of the Dutch Sephardic Jews of Barbados has been told, by whom, and what it leaves out. "Dutch Barbadian" here means the Sephardic families that reached Barbados through the Dutch world, from Amsterdam and Dutch Brazil, and their descendants.

Read with caution. Kasa Baruh's position is that many of the published accounts of the Barbados Jewish past were written in collaboration with the Barbados Jewish Community, which, in Kasa Baruh's view, maintains a policy of "paper genocide" by declaring the Sephardim of Barbados vanished or extinct. The sections on David Raphael de Mercado and after are Kasa Baruh's own historiography: interpretive, argued from a particular standpoint, and presented here as the Dama's account.

The standard account

Jewish settlement in Barbados is usually traced to the 1650s. Abraham de Mercado, a physician, and his son David Raphael de Mercado had lived in Recife, in Dutch Brazil, and reached Barbados around 1655–56. Accounts differ on exactly when the Nidhe Israel congregation began, and some tell the story as a flight from the Portuguese reconquest of Brazil.

David Raphael de Mercado brought sugar-milling technology developed in Recife, a device that turned the Dutch windmill into a sugar factory. In 1679 his name headed the list as the largest Jewish taxpayer in Barbados. His will provided for the wall around the Bridgetown synagogue and cemetery, and he died in 1685.

David Raphael de Mercado, in the Dama's account

David Raphael de Mercado rests as one of the most brilliant, ominous and complicated Portuguese Jews of the 17th century. He operated on the furthest frontier of the Dutch West India Company's expansion into the Americas, a refugee expelled by the Portuguese with the collapse of Dutch Brazil.

When Manasseh ben Israel wrote to Oliver Cromwell asking that Jews be permitted to resettle in England, David Raphael de Mercado and his father were de facto benefactors. They had already been granted travel passes that let them enter Barbados, though his wife, Gracia Baruh Lousada, was not included in that first paperwork. She would nonetheless be present with three other siblings and with hundreds of other Dutch Sephardic Jews gathered by Mercado himself for the infiltration of British trade networks.

This German-Genoese-Dutch-Brazilian Portuguese Jew was closely intertwined with the system of slavery perfected in Barbados: chattel slavery, and the industrialisation of the sugar trade through his powerful sugar-crushing innovation, an advanced windmill that in the Dama's view laid the groundwork for the industrial revolution in England. These windmills allowed Barbados to outstrip other sugar colonies of the British Empire and made it, for a time, the empire's crown jewel. De Mercado knew, as his peers did, that "Dutch Brazil could not survive without the slaves of Angola."

Dutch Brazil capitulated, and by 1654 the Dutch West India Company had massive, unrepayable debts. For the Portuguese Jews this was a disaster, and many would be burned at the stake by the Portuguese Inquisition, which returned with vigour and punished all collaborators. De Mercado had a plan, knowing full well that those windmills were death coffins.

The slave code and the bar on conversion

Dutch Brazil was regarded as a resounding if brief success on the Dutch mainland. It helped wind down the wars against the Iberian kingdoms and gave the Dutch a model for colonial expansion. Portuguese Jews revelled for some twenty years in revenge against the Iberian Catholics for the Inquisition and its unyielding persecution. The penal code, or "slave code," that made Dutch Brazil so lucrative passed from the Dutch Brazilian venture to British Barbados.

When the Dutch Sephardic Jews arrived, the system in Barbados was contractual rather than generational, and included Irish and African indentured servants as well as the enslaved. Some semblance of human consideration existed, since the contracts had to end. According to the Dama, on the advice of Dutch Sephardic Jews, and aided by the legal system codified in the slave code, the British Barbadians began the mass importation of enslaved African labour. For this reason more enslaved people were trafficked to Barbados, and died there, than in the United States.

The Dutch Sephardic Jews considered themselves wise. They forbade anyone of African ancestry from converting to Judaism, so as not to upset the racial order of the budding plantation society, something that was not practised in the Dutch territories. In the Dama's view this would lead to the near annihilation of their people in Barbados, to the expulsion of Sir Isaac Brandon Lopez, and to many descendants of Dutch Sephardic Jews being erased, dispossessed and denied the right to exist as their parents, grandparents and forefathers had.

For his genius, de Mercado was betrayed by the British Barbadians. The colonial authorities enacted a series of antisemitic laws in 1668, a decade after the Jews arrived, and false accusations flew from British merchants. One claim was true: the Dutch Sephardic Jews were trading heavily with Dutch merchants, which was the infrastructure of the Dutch West India Company and the very reason for their presence. Condemned to a ghetto and forced into poverty, they lived in what became known as Jew Street, later Swan Street, where families such as that of John Castello Montefiore lived for generations. It bled into Spry Street, which the Dama says Tom Adams destroyed to build the Central Bank of Barbados, a move Sir Errol Walton Barrow fiercely protested. In the same breath, she says, Adams acquired the property of the Esnoga and sought to destroy it along with the graves inside.

Moses Tolano and the Tolano coin

Moses Tolano was a merchant in Bridgetown, at 33 Swan Street, whose death was recorded in 1852. Slavery was not abolished in the British Empire until 1834, so the date 1830 on his coin suggests an early supporter of freedom. He was a Dutch Sephardic Jew who held two convictions: Jewish emancipation, and the abolition of slavery in Barbados.

He was not alone. Simon Barrow of Bath, a notable New Anglican, freed his slaves without legal compulsion before abolition, and shared the view that slavery was inhumane and morally contradictory. Some Jews who migrated from Barbados to Jamaica, such as the Baruh Lousadas, amassed considerable wealth from the system of racial domination and exploitation.

The Dama reads the coin through a Dutch-Barbadian lens. Jewish emancipation had been achieved a decade earlier, in 1821, and in 1802 all discriminatory laws against Barbadian Jews had been lifted. By 1830, when the coin was minted, the Dutch Sephardic community of Barbados was embroiled in scandals concerning race, hereditary leadership, gender roles and wealth.

The Vestry Bill and Isaac Brandon Lopez

In the Vestry Bill the Jews petitioned to make the Nidhe Israel a vestry. That would have given the Esnoga power equivalent to the Anglican vestries, including political representation and the power to tax those excluded from the Nidhe. It set off a sequence of events that sent the community into what the Dama calls a halachic death spiral, with antisemitic and racial conspiracies from Jews and non-Jews alike.

What happened. Sir Isaac Brandon Lopez was exiled, struck from the full list of Yehudim and embroiled in a racial conspiracy about his "coloured connections." His decorum, financial contributions and esteem had no equal, and he had spent countless nights with his father agitating for Jewish rights. Envy, authoritarianism and abuses of the Mahamad's power alienated Jews who were poor, female, or unwilling to accept exclusion, taxation, or not being emancipated themselves.

Why it happened. The Anglican plantocracy had always seen the Dutch Barbadian Jews as an economic and cultural threat. Jews were pushed to perform whiteness, to maintain strict gender roles, which became a matter of avid debate, and to keep the Esnoga patriarchal in order to appeal to the sensibilities of "White" Christian racial supremacists. A certain Levi, depicted in Laura A. Leibman's scholarship, took matters into his own hands and seems to have "tricked" other Jews into signing a petition against Isaac himself. Some of those who signed were women, and they would regret it. Meanwhile antisemitic conspiracies about the foreignness and inherent evil of Jews persisted in the House of Assembly.

Questions were asked. Who is being emancipated by Jewish emancipation? Simply White Jews, or Black Jews? What are the implications for Black civil rights if a Black Jew is emancipated? Are women entitled to Jewish emancipation?

In the community there were many unmarried women who amassed considerable wealth and ideological sway within powerful institutions and circles, and who often left their wealth to female relatives or friends. The Mahamad saw them as a threat, and Leibman's work on Jewish emancipation in Barbados depicts them more fully. Most Jews were poor, and most of the poor were women, often widows, who benefited from Sedeca (סידיקה).

"Black Jews? But there is only one Black Jew?" At the time at least 10% of the Jewish population was of African descent, though not legally defined as Jews, a barrier Isaac Brandon Lopez hoped to break. In the first explosion of controversy the Esnoga's Mahamad declared that he was no different from other Jews: he had converted in Suriname and was within the fold of Judaism. That was not an acceptable answer for the plantocracy, and the Esnoga succumbed to the pressure.

These coloured Jews were descendants of free coloured women or of enslaved women. Those descended from enslaved women, like the Brandon-Lopez-Gills, were born enslaved and sometimes owned by relatives. The practice is portrayed as a way to protect a relative from a more humiliating or quicker demise, but it fostered a culture in which Sephardic parents and relatives had unquestioned control over the lives, bodies and decisions of their children and younger kin. A Sephardic slave owner could ruin the life of an enslaved person by selling them. These enslaved kin were domestic rather than plantation labourers, which meant a shorter lifespan, and compliance could easily be demanded on those grounds. They were often used as financial leverage over their parents and lovers.

The Vestry Bill passed the Barbadian legislature with anti-African clauses and many more discriminatory stipulations, devastating the Jewish community in the process, only to be vetoed by King George IV. Jewish emancipation was achieved regardless, but slavery did not end. With Dutch Barbadian Eurafrican Jewry destroyed, Moses Tolano minted his coin. To this day there has never been an openly Jewish Dutch Sephardic political representative in Barbados.

Swan Street and the Barbadian merchants

Getting scammed in Swan Street is a canon event. Have you ever been scammed in Swan Street? It turns out that is a certified Dutch Barbadian Sephardic classic. The mercantile culture of Swan Street, formerly Jew Street, was built by Dutch Sephardic Jews such as John Castello Montefiore, the Barrow and Lousada families, the Massiahs, the Nunez and many more, among them Moses Tolano, responsible for the Tolano coin, the pro-emancipation, abolitionist coin that today resides in the Central Bank discussed above.

In the Dama's questioning and informal interviews, the current residents and merchants of Swan Street know nothing of the district's origins, yet she holds it a resounding fact that Dutch Barbadian Jews built the infrastructure and culture that still drives a large share of the city's trade. The upper floors of so many buildings are empty because the ancestors did business downstairs and lived upstairs. The balconies are there because Jewish families in Bridgetown loved evening balcony chats, speaking with each other and with passers-by, which built a strong, tight-knit sense of community. Ay vosh sabiash, the Jews love to chitter!

Swan Street was not only about making money, losing money, getting scammed or running into people you did not want to see. It was also about how families, forcibly ghettoised by antisemites, persevered and brought rare and exotic goods from the far-flung regions of the Naçaõ trade networks. That made the district popular, and when the ancestors decided to sell their properties and emigrate or move to the countryside, buyers appeared without delay because they could see the economic value of what had been built. Despite the brutal racial dynamics of the Esnoga and of plantation society, families, white-passing, intermarried or otherwise, would share an evening conversation, laugh and rely on each other. That is something to be proud of.

The Montefiore Fountain

The Barbadian Government calls the Montefiore Fountain a testament to Barbados' unparalleled heritage, while, the Dama says, maintaining a cultural genocide of Dutch Sephardic Jews. Who commissioned it? The son of John Castello Montefiore, a respected merchant described as free coloured, who for forty years was one of the leading merchants of the city, with a wholesale and retail store in Swan Street (Jew Street) and ownership of Neils Plantation.

The Castello family had free coloured descendants, one of whom the Dama traces directly to Amelia Baruh, her paternal ancestor, through the legacy of British slavery, seemingly as some sort of business partner. The Montefiores were directly related to the Baruh Lousadas, the Barrows, the Rothschilds, the Castellos, the Massiahs, the Lopez and Rodríguez families, and many more prominent Ashkenazi and Dutch Sephardic lineages.

By about 1850 the exile of Sir Isaac Brandon Lopez was already in effect, so in ordinary circumstances John Castello Montefiore and his son would likely not have qualified for Esnoga membership, unless their African ancestry was entirely paternal. Either way, the Dama argues, this strongly challenges the current narrative of the Barbados Jewish Community about who the Jews of Barbados truly were. There were many wealthy Eurafrican Jews in this period, none of whom were entitled to the Nidhe Israel, a policy that she says continues to this day.

The Montefiores served as the primary diplomats of the Western Sephardic world: forming political connections, securing marriages, advocating for oppressed crypto-Jews in Islamic lands and building powerful monuments like this one. Yet history often omits that Afro-Sephardim originated from their presence in Barbados, and that one of the most esteemed, and perhaps hypocritical, monuments in modern Barbados comes from them. These Afro-Sephardim show that a lineage can be both an oppressor and the oppressed.

Deze Duits-Genuees-Nederlands-Braziliaanse Portugese Jood was nauw verweven met het systeem van slavernij dat op Barbados werd geperfectioneerd: plantageslavernij, en de industrialisering van de suikerhandel door zijn krachtige suikerpers-innovatie, een geavanceerde windmolen die volgens de Dama de basis legde voor de industriële revolutie in Engeland. Deze molens lieten Barbados andere suikerkoloniën van het Britse Rijk ver overtreffen en maakten het een tijd lang het kroonjuweel van het rijk. De Mercado wist, net als zijn tijdgenoten, dat "Dutch Brazil could not survive without the slaves of Angola."

Nederlands Brazilië capituleerde, en in 1654 had de Nederlandse West-Indische Compagnie enorme, onafbetaalbare schulden. Voor de Portugese Joden was dit een ramp, en velen zouden op de brandstapel eindigen door de Portugese Inquisitie, die met kracht terugkeerde en alle collaborateurs strafte. De Mercado had een plan, wetende dat die windmolens doodskisten waren.

De slavencode en het verbod op bekering

Nederlands Brazilië werd op het Nederlandse vasteland gezien als een grootse, zij het korte, succesvolle onderneming. Het hielp de oorlogen tegen de Iberische koninkrijken af te bouwen en gaf de Nederlanders een model voor koloniale expansie. Portugese Joden vierden zo'n twintig jaar feest in wraak op de Iberische katholieken voor de Inquisitie en de meedogenloze vervolging. Het strafrecht, of de "slavencode", dat Nederlands Brazilië zo winstgevend maakte, ging van de Nederlands-Braziliaanse onderneming over naar Brits Barbados.

Toen de Nederlandse sefardische Joden aankwamen, was het systeem op Barbados contractueel in plaats van generatiegebonden en omvatte het Ierse en Afrikaanse contractarbeiders en tot slaaf gemaakten. Er was nog enige menselijke consideratie, want de contracten moesten immers eindigen. Volgens de Dama begonnen de Britse Barbadianen op advies van Nederlandse sefardische Joden, geholpen door het rechtssysteem dat in de slavencode was vastgelegd, aan de massale invoer van tot slaaf gemaakte Afrikaanse arbeiders. Daarom werden meer tot slaaf gemaakten naar Barbados gesmokkeld, en stierven daar, dan in de Verenigde Staten.

De Nederlandse sefardische Joden achtten zichzelf wijs. Zij verboden iedereen van Afrikaanse afkomst zich tot het jodendom te bekeren, om de raciale orde van de ontluikende plantagesamenleving niet te verstoren, iets wat in de Nederlandse gebieden niet gebeurde. Volgens de Dama zou dit leiden tot de bijna-vernietiging van hun volk op Barbados, tot de verbanning van Sir Isaac Brandon Lopez, en ertoe dat veel nakomelingen van Nederlandse sefardische Joden werden uitgewist, onteigend en het recht werd ontzegd te bestaan zoals hun ouders, grootouders en voorvaderen.

Voor zijn genialiteit werd De Mercado verraden door de Britse Barbadianen. De koloniale autoriteiten voerden in 1668, een decennium na aankomst van de Joden, een reeks antisemitische wetten in, en valse beschuldigingen vlogen van Britse kooplieden. Eén bewering was waar: de Nederlandse sefardische Joden dreven zwaar handel met Nederlandse kooplieden, wat de infrastructuur van de Nederlandse West-Indische Compagnie was en de reden van hun aanwezigheid. Veroordeeld tot een getto en tot armoede gedwongen, woonden zij in wat bekend werd als Jew Street, later Swan Street, waar families als die van John Castello Montefiore generaties lang woonden. Het liep over in Spry Street, die volgens de Dama door Tom Adams werd verwoest om de Centrale Bank van Barbados te bouwen, wat Sir Errol Walton Barrow fel bestreed. In één adem, zegt zij, verwierf Adams het bezit van de Esnoga en probeerde hij die samen met de graven erin te vernietigen.

Moses Tolano en de Tolano-munt

Moses Tolano was een koopman in Bridgetown, aan Swan Street 33, wiens overlijden in 1852 werd geregistreerd. Slavernij werd in het Britse Rijk pas in 1834 afgeschaft, dus de datum 1830 op zijn munt wijst op een vroege voorstander van vrijheid. Hij was een Nederlandse sefardische Jood met twee overtuigingen: joodse emancipatie, en de afschaffing van slavernij op Barbados.

Hij stond niet alleen. Simon Barrow van Bath, een gezien Nieuw-Anglicaan, bevrijdde zijn slaven zonder wettelijke verplichting vóór de afschaffing en deelde de opvatting dat slavernij onmenselijk en moreel tegenstrijdig was. Sommige Joden die van Barbados naar Jamaica trokken, zoals de Baruh Lousada's, vergaarden aanzienlijke rijkdom uit het systeem van raciale overheersing en uitbuiting.

De Dama leest de munt door een Nederlands-Barbadiaanse bril. Joodse emancipatie was een decennium eerder, in 1821, bereikt, en in 1802 waren alle discriminerende wetten tegen Barbadiaanse Joden opgeheven. Toen de munt in 1830 werd geslagen, was de Nederlands-sefardische gemeenschap van Barbados verstrikt in schandalen over ras, erfelijk leiderschap, genderrollen en rijkdom.

De Vestry Bill en Isaac Brandon Lopez

In de Vestry Bill verzochten de Joden om Nidhe Israel tot vestry te maken. Dat zou de Esnoga macht hebben gegeven gelijk aan de anglicaanse vestry's, inclusief politieke vertegenwoordiging en de bevoegdheid om hen die van de Nidhe waren uitgesloten te belasten. Het zette een reeks gebeurtenissen in gang die de gemeenschap in wat de Dama een halachische neerwaartse spiraal noemt stortte, met antisemitische en raciale samenzweringen van Joden en niet-Joden.

Wat er gebeurde. Sir Isaac Brandon Lopez werd verbannen, van de volledige lijst van Yehudim geschrapt en verwikkeld in een raciale samenzwering over zijn "gekleurde connecties". Zijn welvoeglijkheid, financiële bijdragen en aanzien waren ongeëvenaard, en hij had talloze nachten met zijn vader gestreden voor joodse rechten. Afgunst, autoritair gedrag en misbruik van de macht van de Mahamad vervreemdden Joden die arm, vrouw, of niet bereid waren uitsluiting, belasting of zelf niet geëmancipeerd te zijn te accepteren.

Waarom het gebeurde. De anglicaanse plantocratie had de Nederlands-Barbadiaanse Joden altijd gezien als een economische en culturele bedreiging. Joden werden gedwongen blank te spelen, strikte genderrollen aan te houden, wat onderwerp van felle discussie werd, en de Esnoga patriarchaal te houden om aan te sluiten bij de opvattingen van "blanke" christelijke raciale suprematisten. Een zekere Levi, uitgebeeld in het werk van Laura A. Leibman, nam het heft in eigen handen en lijkt andere Joden te hebben "misleid" om een petitie tegen Isaac zelf te ondertekenen. Sommige ondertekenaars waren vrouwen, en zij zouden er spijt van krijgen. Intussen bleven antisemitische samenzweringen over het vreemd zijn en het inherente kwaad van Joden aanhouden in het House of Assembly.

Er werden vragen gesteld. Wie wordt geëmancipeerd door joodse emancipatie? Slechts blanke Joden, of ook Zwarte Joden? Wat zijn de gevolgen voor Zwarte burgerrechten als een Zwarte Jood wordt geëmancipeerd? Hebben vrouwen recht op joodse emancipatie?

In de gemeenschap waren veel ongehuwde vrouwen die aanzienlijke rijkdom en ideologische invloed binnen machtige instellingen en kringen opbouwden en vaak hun vermogen naliet aan vrouwelijke verwanten of vriendinnen. De Mahamad zag hen als een bedreiging, en Leibmans werk over joodse emancipatie op Barbados beschrijft hen uitvoeriger. De meeste Joden waren arm, en de meeste armen waren vrouwen, vaak weduwen, die steun kregen van Sedeca (סידיקה).

"Zwarte Joden? Er is toch maar één Zwarte Jood?" Destijds was minstens 10% van de joodse bevolking van Afrikaanse afkomst, hoewel zij wettelijk niet als Joden golden, een barrière die Isaac Brandon Lopez hoopte te doorbreken. In de eerste uitbarsting van controverse verklaarde de Mahamad van de Esnoga dat hij niet verschilde van andere Joden: hij was in Suriname bekeerd en maakte deel uit van het jodendom. Dat was voor de plantocratie geen aanvaardbaar antwoord, en de Esnoga bezweek onder de druk.

Deze gekleurde Joden waren nakomelingen van vrije gekleurde vrouwen of van tot slaaf gemaakte vrouwen. Wie van tot slaaf gemaakte vrouwen afstamde, zoals de Brandon-Lopez-Gills, werd slaaf geboren en soms bezeten door verwanten. De praktijk wordt voorgesteld als een manier om een verwant te beschermen tegen een vernederender of sneller einde, maar zij kweekte een cultuur waarin sefardische ouders en verwanten onbetwiste controle hadden over het leven, het lichaam en de beslissingen van hun kinderen en jongere verwanten. Een sefardische slavenhouder kon het leven van een tot slaaf gemaakte verwoesten door die te verkopen. Deze tot slaaf gemaakte verwanten waren huisbedienden, geen plantagearbeiders, wat een kortere levensverwachting betekende, en gehoorzaamheid kon op die gronden gemakkelijk worden afgedwongen. Zij werden vaak gebruikt als financieel drukmiddel tegen hun ouders en geliefden.

De Vestry Bill werd door de Barbadiaanse wetgevende macht aangenomen met anti-Afrikaanse clausules en nog veel meer discriminerende bepalingen, wat de joodse gemeenschap verwoestte, om vervolgens door koning George IV te worden afgewezen. Joodse emancipatie werd toch bereikt, maar slavernij eindigde niet. Nu het Nederlands-Barbadiaanse eurafrikaanse jodendom was vernietigd, sloeg Moses Tolano zijn munt. Why communal consensus matters

The history of the Dutch Barbadian Sephardim is contested ground. Institutions, publishers, tourism boards and well-meaning outsiders increasingly tell it for us, and a story told without its people can be sold: turned into heritage branding, museum copy, genealogy tourism and "authentic" merchandise that profit from the community's name while excluding or erasing its living descendants. That is commodification. Gentrification works on identity in the same way: the neighbourhood of memory is renovated for newcomers, its original residents are priced out of their own history, and the paper trail is rewritten so that they seem never to have lived there.

Communal consensus is how the Dutch Barbadian nation safeguards itself against both. When the community itself agrees what its history says, who may speak to it, and on what evidence, that history cannot be quietly swapped for a more convenient one. A record checked by the people it describes is harder to commodify, and harder to declare extinct.

Sanction

This sanction is not meant to control. It exists to create consensus, understanding and an ethical standard of communal historiography: a shared practice of sourcing claims, naming uncertainty, crediting those whose records and memories the history rests on, and correcting errors openly. Disagreement stays welcome (the forum is open); the aim is that disagreements are settled by evidence and conversation, not by whoever has the most bandwidth.

What follows is the communally sanctioned and verified history of the Barbadian Jews.

The history of the Jews in Barbados goes back to the 1600s. A Jewish population has been in Barbados continually since 1654. These Jews who arrived in Barbados in the mid-17th century were known as Conversos, Marranos and even Crypto-Jews.

The French writer and historian Jacques Attali describes these Conversos as people who blended Christian and Jewish beliefs, raised in doubt between two religions and rejecting fixed definitions of what is real, just or beautiful. In his view (Les Juifs, le Monde et l'Argent, 2002) that outlook helped them invent the scientific mind and made them among the most emancipated minds of their time.

Origins

The Jewish arrival in Barbados is a direct consequence of the Spanish Inquisition, specifically the Alhambra Decree. In 1492, some Sephardic Jews had fled the persecution in the Iberian Peninsula for Brazil, where they remained until the 17th century. In Barbados, there are two divisions between “Dutch Sephardic Jews”.

Those being families who resided in Dutch Brazil and were expelled as a consequence of the Portuguese recapture, such as the Mercados, Nevarro and Valverde and those who came from Amsterdam through Britain, such as the Baruch Lousada (Barrow Lousada), Brandon. The Massiah Family came from Morocco through Amsterdam, then Britain. The Massiahs held many ties with the De Pezas, Baruch Lousada, and many other families.

These families held strong genealogical ties. The following labels may apply: “Dutch Brasilian”, “Dutch Italian”, “Dutch German”, “Dutch Moroccan”, all beneath the banner of “Dutch Sephardi” Jews of la Nação.

In 1654~, 300 Dutch Sephardi Jews settled on the island, the first account of Jewish people settling in Barbados. This number would be confirmed by 1680.

The “Dutch Brasilian Jews” in Brazil were forced to flee once again from what was formerly Dutch Brazil (specifically Recife, where there existed a large Jewish community) after it was captured in 1654 by the Inquisitorial persecuting Portuguese colonisers who were consolidating their hold over all of Brazil. They fled back to Amsterdam, then to London to seek official clearance to settle in Barbados, which they were granted in 1654. Families begin gathering in Amsterdam.

The early Barbadian population was increased from two other sources. In 1664, the Jewish settlement at Cayenne was dissolved, and a few moved to Barbados. In 1674, several people also moved from Suriname following its surrender to the Dutch; the majority of these were deported to Jamaica, but a few managed to stay in Barbados. Two communities were established over time, the first being centred around the Nidhe Israel Synagogue in the Capital, Bridgetown, and a smaller one in northern Speightstown. In Bridgetown, out of a total of 404 householders, 54 were Jews.

The name Barbados, meaning ‘the bearded ones’ officially comes from the island’s bearded fig tree, but local Historians doubt this version as there are very few of those trees on the island (he counted 50) and rather suggest the hypothesis (while admitting having no physical evidence) that ‘the bearded ones’ were actually the Sephardi Jews who first inhabited the island.

Economic success, the resulting discrimination

Unlike the case in Suriname, very few of the Rabbinic Barbadian Sephardim were plantation owners. Given the small size of Barbados, all the arable land was already occupied by the 1660s. Many Jewish settlers engaged in sugar and coffee cultivation. While the British government considered Jews to be good businessmen and tradesmen, the British merchants accused them of committing illegal business transactions.

Jews were accused of trading more frequently with the Dutch than the British merchants. In 1661, three Jewish traders in Barbados requested to establish trade routes between Barbados and the Dutch Colony of Suriname; through this enterprise, the Jews gained much wealth, but further irritated the British merchants.

By 1679, nearly 300 Dutch Sephardi Jews lived in Barbados.

On October 23, 1668, the Jews of Barbados were banned from all forms of trade. Jews were forbidden from employing Christians, limited from purchasing more than one male slave, and were forced to live in a Jewish Ghetto in Bridgetown. They could not own more than three acres of land and three slaves.

During the 18th century, the Jewish community of Barbados continued to grow and become financially successful, although the Jewish congregation in Speightstown suffered a pogrom wherein the Jews were temporarily chased out of town. The 1750 census recorded 800 Jews out of the 18,000 White inhabitants of the island. It is unknown how many Eurafrican Jews there were, as they were not legally Jewish, this contributes to the unknown Sephardic demographic contribution in Barbados.

All the discriminatory laws were removed by 1802, by the colonial government of Barbados and in 1820 the British Parliament also repealed the discriminatory laws. The Speightstown Esnoga was destroyed in the Hurricane of 1831 and was never rebuilt. To this day, the location of this Esnoga — and its records at the point of destruction is a mystery.

Contribution to the Sugar Trade

The Jewish refugees brought their expertise in the production and cultivation of sugarcane and coffee from Dutch Brazil, expertise which contributed to the development of Barbados as a major producer of sugar, in tandem with the Slave Code the Dutch Sephardim installed within Dutch Brazil. They brought new technological methods to the sugar industry and helped the island become economically established.

In short, David Raphael de Mercado’s windmill invention and the Slavery-based penal code of Dutch Brasil directly influenced the industrialisation of the Sugar trade. Due to this, Barbados “punched” far above its weight as a sugar-producing colony; conversely, Brazil’s decline as a sugar-trading colony would immediately follow the exit of these Dutch Sephardi Jews. It would rise in value once more when Gold was found, but not for its sugar exportation.

The British would export this model, empowered by migrations of Dutch Sephardi Jews, which led to Jamaica’s rise as a Sugar Trading Colony and eventually, members of the Barrow Lousada Family of Barbados being ennobled as Dukes in Europe.

Among the first Jewish settlers in Barbados was David Rafael de Mercado, who had previously developed in Recife a way to industrialise windmills.

Decline and revival

For over two hundred years, the Barbadian Jews had been legally marginalised by antisemitic laws, christian supremacy, and even a pogrom which destroyed the Speightstown Esnoga in 1739, all the while the prohibition on the conversion of those of African descent took its toll on the legally Jewish population, the Dutch Sephardim were quickly becoming unable to reproduce their institutional members, many would leave seeking more tolerant environments, on the basis of their race, legal marginalisation, or better opportunity elsewhere.

In 1831, the island was devastated by a hurricane that severely damaged the economy and by 1848 the legally Jewish population had seen a significant decline in numbers, this coincided with the abolition of slavery and the infamous Vestry Bill conflict. The storm had killed 1,500 people, destroyed the original Nidhe Israel Synagogue, and flattened most of the island, many records were lost and the Speightstown Esnoga was destroyed a second time, never to be rebuilt, lost to time, and its records a mystery.

Emigration, and coercive Catholic and Anglican marriage vows eventually took their toll on the legally Jewish population of the island, these vows forced Jewish men and women alike to raise their children Catholic or Anglican, and in some instances–demanded baptism, which often led to Anglicisation; the legally Jewish population has ceased to be able to reproduce itself in any capacity by 1929 when it is said that the last of the legally Jewish descendants of the Brazilian Jews left the island, but the Jewishness of descendants remained biologically and culturally, best exemplified by the “numerous” Barrow “mulattos”, remained.

There are many “artefacts” and “historical facts” concerning these Dutch Sephardic Jews around Bridgetown. The community’s synagogue fell into a state of disuse due to the historical legacy of Intra-Jewish Slavery beneath British Colonialism, with the exile of the “Jew by inclination”, son of the Parnas and wealthiest Jew in the Island, Isaac Brandon Lopez, came with the definitive “silencing” of Coloured Barbadian Dutch Sephardi Jewry. Despite this, Dutch Sephardic descendants would participate in and suffer casualties in World War II. An example of one descendant who went to battle against the Nazi Regime was Sir Errol Walton Barrow.

A legally Jewish presence returned to the island in the aftermath of World War II, in the form of Ashkenazi Jewish refugees from Eastern Europe, these Jews became the successors of and inheritors of the Nidhei Israel and Dutch Sephardic institutional representation in Barbados. In 1968, there were about 80 Rabbinic Jews out of a total population of 251,000. Although small, the legally Jewish community has taken steps to preserve Dutch Barbadian Sephardic and Barbadian Ashkenazi heritage: for instance, in maintaining a functioning synagogue, the Nidhe Israel Synagogue, in the capital city Bridgetown. In January, 2008, the Nidhe Israel Museum was opened. The museum tells the story of the legally Jewish Barbadian Sephardic and Ashkenazi communities. Also in 2008, the archaeology of the yard of the seventeenth-century synagogue was investigated by historians/archaeologists and students from the University of the West Indies. Though the focus is on the long destroyed rabbi’s house, excavations have revealed the intact 17th-century mikveh, which was constructed over a still-running freshwater spring.

Oorsprong

De joodse aankomst op Barbados is een direct gevolg van de Spaanse Inquisitie, met name het Alhambra-edict. In 1492 waren sommige sefardische Joden voor de vervolging op het Iberisch schiereiland naar Brazilië gevlucht, waar zij tot de 17e eeuw bleven. Op Barbados zijn er twee groepen "Nederlandse sefardische Joden".

Dat zijn families die in Nederlands Brazilië woonden en na de Portugese herovering werden verdreven, zoals de Mercado's, Nevarro en Valverde, en families die via Amsterdam en Groot-Brittannië kwamen, zoals de Baruch Lousada (Barrow Lousada) en Brandon. De familie Massiah kwam uit Marokko via Amsterdam en vervolgens Groot-Brittannië. De Massiahs hadden veel banden met de De Pezas, de Baruch Lousada's en vele andere families.

Deze families onderhielden sterke genealogische banden. De volgende benamingen kunnen van toepassing zijn: "Nederlands-Braziliaans", "Nederlands-Italiaans", "Nederlands-Duits", "Nederlands-Marokkaans", allemaal onder de vlag van de "Nederlandse sefardische" Joden van la Nação.

Rond 1654 vestigden zich 300 Nederlandse sefardische Joden op het eiland, het eerste bericht over joodse vestiging op Barbados. Dit aantal zou in 1680 worden bevestigd.

De "Nederlands-Braziliaanse Joden" in Brazilië moesten opnieuw vluchten uit het voormalige Nederlands Brazilië (met name Recife, waar een grote joodse gemeenschap bestond) nadat het in 1654 was ingenomen door de inquisitoriale, vervolgende Portugese kolonisten die hun greep op heel Brazilië verstevigden. Zij vluchtten terug naar Amsterdam en vervolgens naar Londen om officiële toestemming te vragen zich op Barbados te vestigen, die zij in 1654 kregen. Families begonnen zich in Amsterdam te verzamelen.

De vroege Barbadiaanse bevolking werd uit twee andere bronnen aangevuld. In 1664 werd de joodse nederzetting in Cayenne ontbonden en enkelen trokken naar Barbados. In 1674 verhuisden ook enkele mensen uit Suriname na de overgave aan de Nederlanders; de meesten werden naar Jamaica gedeporteerd, maar enkelen bleven op Barbados. In de loop der tijd ontstonden twee gemeenschappen: de eerste rond de synagoge Nidhe Israel in de hoofdstad Bridgetown en een kleinere in Speightstown in het noorden. In Bridgetown waren van de 404 huishoudens 54 joods.

De naam Barbados, "de gebaarden", komt officieel van de gebaarde vijgenboom van het eiland, maar lokale historici betwijfelen dit omdat er maar weinig van die bomen op het eiland zijn (hij telde er 50). Zij stellen voor, zonder fysiek bewijs te hebben, dat "de gebaarden" in werkelijkheid de sefardische Joden waren die het eiland het eerst bewoonden.

Economisch succes en de daaruit voortvloeiende discriminatie

Anders dan in Suriname waren zeer weinig van de rabbijnse Barbadiaanse sefardim plantage-eigenaren. Door de kleine omvang van Barbados was in de jaren 1660 al het bouwland bezet. Veel joodse kolonisten deden aan suiker- en koffieteelt. De Britse regering beschouwde Joden als goede zakenlieden en handelaren, maar Britse kooplieden beschuldigden hen van illegale transacties.

Joden werden ervan beschuldigd vaker met de Nederlanders te handelen dan Britse kooplieden. In 1661 verzochten drie joodse handelaren op Barbados om handelsroutes te openen tussen Barbados en de Nederlandse kolonie Suriname; hierdoor verwierven de Joden veel rijkdom, maar ergerden zij de Britse kooplieden verder.

Tegen 1679 woonden bijna 300 Nederlandse sefardische Joden op Barbados.

Op 23 oktober 1668 werd de Joden van Barbados elke vorm van handel verboden. Joden mochten geen christenen in dienst nemen, mochten niet meer dan één mannelijke slaaf kopen en werden gedwongen in een joods getto in Bridgetown te wonen. Zij mochten niet meer dan drie acres land en drie slaven bezitten.

In de 18e eeuw bleef de joodse gemeenschap van Barbados groeien en financieel succesvol worden, hoewel de gemeente in Speightstown een pogrom onderging waarbij de Joden tijdelijk de stad uit werden gejaagd. De volkstelling van 1750 registreerde 800 Joden onder de 18.000 blanke inwoners van het eiland. Het is onbekend hoeveel eurafrikaanse Joden er waren, omdat zij wettelijk niet joods waren; dit draagt bij aan de onbekende sefardische demografische bijdrage op Barbados.

Alle discriminerende wetten werden tegen 1802 door de koloniale regering van Barbados opgeheven en in 1820 trok ook het Britse parlement de discriminerende wetten in. De Esnoga van Speightstown werd verwoest door de orkaan van 1831 en nooit herbouwd. Bijdrage aan de suikerhandel

De joodse vluchtelingen brachten hun expertise in de productie en teelt van suikerriet en koffie uit Nederlands Brazilië mee, expertise die bijdroeg aan de ontwikkeling van Barbados tot grote suikerproducent, samen met de slavencode die de Nederlandse sefardim in Nederlands Brazilië hadden ingevoerd. Zij brachten nieuwe technologische methoden naar de suikerindustrie en hielpen het eiland economisch te vestigen.

Kortom, David Raphael de Mercado's windmolenuitvinding en de op slavernij gebaseerde strafcode van Nederlands Brazilië beïnvloedden direct de industrialisering van de suikerhandel. Daardoor presteerde Barbados als suikerkolonie ver boven zijn gewicht; omgekeerd volgde Brazilië's neergang als suikerkolonie onmiddellijk op het vertrek van deze Nederlandse sefardische Joden. De waarde ervan steeg opnieuw toen er goud werd gevonden, maar niet door suikerexport.

De Britten exporteerden dit model, gesteund door migraties van Nederlandse sefardische Joden, wat leidde tot Jamaica's opkomst als suikerkolonie en uiteindelijk tot de verheffing van leden van de familie Barrow Lousada van Barbados tot hertogen in Europa.

Onder de eerste joodse kolonisten op Barbados was David Rafael de Mercado, die eerder in Recife een manier had ontwikkeld om windmolens te industrialiseren.

Neergang en herleving

Ruim twee eeuwen lang werden de Barbadiaanse Joden wettelijk gemarginaliseerd door antisemitische wetten, christelijke suprematie en zelfs een pogrom die in 1739 de Esnoga van Speightstown verwoestte, terwijl het verbod op bekering van mensen van Afrikaanse afkomst zijn tol eiste van de wettelijk joodse bevolking. De Nederlandse sefardim konden hun institutionele leden nauwelijks meer vervangen en velen vertrokken op zoek naar verdraagzamere omgevingen, vanwege hun ras, wettelijke marginalisering of betere kansen elders.

In 1831 werd het eiland verwoest door een orkaan die de economie zwaar beschadigde, en tegen 1848 was de wettelijk joodse bevolking sterk afgenomen; dit viel samen met de afschaffing van de slavernij en het beruchte Vestry Bill-conflict. De storm doodde 1.500 mensen, verwoestte de oorspronkelijke synagoge Nidhe Israel en maakte het grootste deel van het eiland met de grond gelijk. Veel archieven gingen verloren en de Esnoga van Speightstown werd voor de tweede keer verwoest, nooit herbouwd, verloren in de tijd, met een raadselachtig archief.

Emigratie en dwingende katholieke en anglicaanse huwelijksbeloften eisten uiteindelijk hun tol van de wettelijk joodse bevolking. Deze beloften dwongen joodse mannen en vrouwen hun kinderen katholiek of anglicaans op te voeden en eisten soms de doop, wat vaak tot verengelsing leidde. De wettelijk joodse bevolking kon zich in 1929 op geen enkele manier meer voortplanten, toen naar verluidt de laatste wettelijk joodse nakomelingen van de Braziliaanse Joden het eiland verlieten, maar het jood-zijn van nakomelingen bleef biologisch en cultureel bestaan, het best geïllustreerd door de "talrijke" Barrow-"mulatten".

Er zijn veel "artefacten" en "historische feiten" over deze Nederlandse sefardische Joden in Bridgetown. Een van de nakomelingen die ten strijde trok tegen het naziregime was Sir Errol Walton Barrow.

Na de Tweede Wereldoorlog keerde een wettelijk joodse aanwezigheid terug op het eiland, in de vorm van asjkenazische joodse vluchtelingen uit Oost-Europa. Zij werden de opvolgers en erfgenamen van Nidhei Israel en van de institutionele vertegenwoordiging van de Nederlandse sefardim op Barbados. In 1968 waren er ongeveer 80 rabbijnse Joden op een totale bevolking van 251.000. Hoewel klein, heeft de wettelijk joodse gemeenschap stappen gezet om het Nederlands-Barbadiaanse sefardische en Barbadiaanse asjkenazische erfgoed te behouden, bijvoorbeeld door een functionerende synagoge te onderhouden, Nidhe Israel in de hoofdstad Bridgetown. The Slave Names Conspiracy

The slave names conspiracy is a form of anti-Eurafrican myth-making influenced by the adoption habits of surnames by African Americans. This form of conspiracy claims that the descendants of Sephardic Jewish lineages in Barbados “simply adopted slave names”.

Contemporary scholarship recognises that the majority of Barbadian Jews were not plantation owners, but merchants, who, in the majority, had domestic slaves, some of whom were ethnic jews from related families.

While of course, there were some exceptions to the trend, as the notable Gill-Lopez-Rodrigues-Brandon Clan.

Jerome S. Handler and JoAnn Jacoby's study of slave naming in Barbados, 1650–1830 (The William and Mary Quarterly, 1996) finds that only a tiny fraction of the enslaved were manumitted, far below one percent, and fewer still bought their own freedom. Enslaved people with surnames showed no clear tendency to bear the names of their owners or managers. Jewish antizionism

When Raven asked about Jewish antizionism, De Dama acknowledged that Jewish opposition to Israel can arise from different motivations. However, they argued that some Jewish antizionist environments can involve political pressure, internalised antisemitism, misinformation or social exclusion. De Dama also argued that antizionism can sometimes be used as a vehicle for antisemitism, while recognising that criticism of Israel should not automatically be equated with antisemitism.

Their political boundary is nevertheless clear: Israel's continued existence is non-negotiable within their Zionism. The objective, De Dama argued, should be to make Israel "more," not less, to reform and improve it rather than abolish it.

Netanyahu and political power

De Dama expressed an ambivalent view of Benjamin Netanyahu. They described him as experienced, pragmatic, strategically capable and effective in pursuing wartime objectives, while expressing concern that his government could move toward autocratic forms of power. De Dama acknowledged that crises can produce pressure for executive consolidation, but argued that such power becomes unacceptable when it serves an individual rather than the preservation of the state and its democratic institutions.

De Dama therefore distinguished between strategic effectiveness and political legitimacy, while explicitly rejecting figures such as Itamar Ben-Gvir and other right-wing extremist factions as incompatible with the inclusive character of Decolonial Zionism.

Disability and the ethics of community

Finally, De Dama described disability as a universal human concern. People may become disabled through birth, neurological difference, trauma or war. For De Dama, communities therefore have an ethical obligation to accommodate and support those carrying physical, neurological or psychological scars. Disability rights and inclusion should consequently be treated not as optional policies but as ethical standards that all nations, including Israel, should meet.

The Decolonial Zionist position

Taken together, De Dama di Louzada's conversation with Raven presents Decolonial Zionism as an attempt to reconcile Jewish national liberation with universal minority liberation. Its principles are:

  • Jewish ancestral return.
  • Palestinian self-determination.
  • Minority dignity and security.
  • Queer and transgender liberation.
  • Disability inclusion.
  • Neurodivergent civic participation.
  • Economic empowerment.
  • Meaningful relationships between peoples and their ancestral homelands.

For De Dama, the purpose of Decolonial Zionism is not simply to preserve an existing political order, but to transform it through advocacy, activism, intellectual rigour and an uncompromising pursuit of justice. Their central proposition is ultimately that Jewish liberation must survive alongside the liberation and dignity of other peoples.

Dama di Louzada over Decoloniaal Zionisme

In een interview met Raven S.G. Fridmar beschreef Dama di Louzada hun Decoloniaal Zionisme als een verlengstuk van hun filosofische, theologische en ethische wereldbeeld. Voor De Dama bieden recht, wet, politiek, geschiedenis en filosofie de middelen om de systemen te begrijpen en te veranderen die menselijke veiligheid, waardigheid en empowerment bepalen.

Het Decoloniaal Zionisme van De Dama stelt daarom minderheidsbevrijding, zelfbeschikking, waardigheid en economische empowerment centraal. Het breidt deze principes uit naar het joodse volk, Palestijnen, Koerden, Oekraïners, queer mensen, vrouwen, mensen met een beperking, werknemers en andere gemarginaliseerde gemeenschappen.

Israël en voorouderlijke terugkeer

De Dama ziet Israël als een plaats van voorouderlijke terugkeer en bevrijding voor het joodse volk, ongeacht hun positie binnen hedendaagse religieuze of joods-juridische structuren. Tegelijkertijd stelt De Dama dat het principe van voorouderlijke terugkeer niet uitsluitend aan Joden kan toebehoren. Ook andere volkeren zouden het recht moeten hebben terug te keren naar hun voorouderlijke thuislanden, of daar op betekenisvolle wijze mee verbonden te zijn.

Dit bredere principe onderbouwt De Dama's steun voor pan-Afrikanisme, pan-Europeanisme, Caribische eenwording en wereldburgerschap. Hun kernstelling is dat joodse bevrijding en de bevrijding van andere volkeren elkaar niet hoeven uit te sluiten.

Joodse, autistische en queer ervaring

De Dama vertelde Raven dat hun Decoloniaal Zionisme niet los te zien is van hun ervaringen als Joods, autistisch en queer. Zij benadrukten vooral de uitsluiting van mensen met niet-zichtbare beperkingen en betoogden dat neurodivergente mensen in staat moeten worden gesteld deel te nemen aan het burgerlijk leven, ook in cruciale terreinen als defensie en veiligheid.

Vanuit persoonlijke ervaring beschreef De Dama hoe moeilijk het is dat hun capaciteiten worden betwijfeld vanwege autisme, ook wanneer zij de verwachtingen overtreffen. Hun standpunt is dat een beperking niet bepalend mag zijn voor iemands veronderstelde vermogen om bij te dragen.

Hetzelfde principe geldt voor queer identiteit. De Dama beschreef "het Queer Lichaam" als even goddelijk als elk ander en verbond de vervolging van transgender mensen met bredere geschiedenissen van kolonialisme, religieuze onverdraagzaamheid en institutioneel falen. Voor De Dama zijn queer bevrijding en inclusie van mensen met een beperking daarom integraal onderdeel van hun Decoloniaal Zionisme, en geen aparte politieke kwesties.

Palestina, Hamas en de oorlog

Over de oorlog tussen Israël en Hamas pleitte De Dama voor verantwoording binnen alle politieke gemeenschappen, ook binnen Israël en onder pro-Palestijnse, christelijke en MAGA-bewegingen. De Dama sprak in het bijzonder zorg uit over antisemitisme binnen delen van het hedendaagse pro-Palestijnse discours en over wat zij zagen als de normalisering of romantisering van Hamas.

Tegelijkertijd maakte De Dama onderscheid tussen Palestijnse zelfbeschikking en Hamas. Palestijnen behouden volgens hen een legitiem recht op zelfbeschikking, terwijl Hamas' jihadistische ideologie en politieke geweld fundamenteel onverenigbaar blijven met De Dama's ethische opvatting van bevrijding. Als westers sefardiër beschreef De Dama ook westerse ethische tradities als een belangrijke invloed op hun politieke wereldbeeld.

Joods antizionisme

Toen Raven vroeg naar joods antizionisme, erkende De Dama dat joods verzet tegen Israël uit verschillende motieven kan voortkomen. Wel betoogden zij dat sommige joodse antizionistische omgevingen politieke druk, geïnternaliseerd antisemitisme, desinformatie of sociale uitsluiting kunnen inhouden. De Dama stelde ook dat antizionisme soms als voertuig voor antisemitisme kan dienen, terwijl zij erkennen dat kritiek op Israël niet automatisch gelijkgesteld mag worden met antisemitisme.

Hun politieke grens is niettemin duidelijk: het voortbestaan van Israël is binnen hun zionisme niet onderhandelbaar. Het doel, betoogde De Dama, moet zijn Israël "meer" te maken, niet minder: het te hervormen en te verbeteren in plaats van af te schaffen.

Netanyahu en politieke macht

De Dama uitte een ambivalente visie op Benjamin Netanyahu. Zij beschreven hem als ervaren, pragmatisch, strategisch bekwaam en effectief in het nastreven van oorlogsdoelen, terwijl zij zorg uitten dat zijn regering naar autocratische machtsvormen zou kunnen opschuiven. De Dama erkende dat crises druk kunnen veroorzaken voor consolidatie van de uitvoerende macht, maar betoogde dat zulke macht onaanvaardbaar wordt wanneer zij een individu dient in plaats van het behoud van de staat en haar democratische instellingen.

De Dama maakte daarom onderscheid tussen strategische effectiviteit en politieke legitimiteit, en verwierp uitdrukkelijk figuren als Itamar Ben-Gvir en andere extreemrechtse groepen als onverenigbaar met het inclusieve karakter van Decoloniaal Zionisme.

Beperking en de ethiek van gemeenschap

Tot slot beschreef De Dama beperking als een universele menselijke zorg. Mensen kunnen een beperking krijgen door geboorte, neurologisch verschil, trauma of oorlog. Voor De Dama hebben gemeenschappen daarom een ethische plicht om hen die fysieke, neurologische of psychische littekens dragen op te vangen en te ondersteunen. Rechten en inclusie van mensen met een beperking moeten dan ook niet als optioneel beleid worden behandeld, maar als ethische normen waaraan alle naties, ook Israël, moeten voldoen.

De decolonial-zionistische positie

Alles bijeen presenteert het gesprek van Dama di Louzada met Raven het Decoloniaal Zionisme als een poging om joodse nationale bevrijding te verzoenen met universele minderheidsbevrijding. De principes zijn:

  • Joodse voorouderlijke terugkeer.
  • Palestijnse zelfbeschikking.
  • Waardigheid en veiligheid van minderheden.
  • Queer en transgender bevrijding.
  • Inclusie van mensen met een beperking.
  • Burgerlijke participatie van neurodivergente mensen.
  • Economische empowerment.
  • Betekenisvolle relaties tussen volkeren en hun voorouderlijke thuislanden.

Voor De Dama is het doel van Decoloniaal Zionisme niet alleen om een bestaande politieke orde te behouden, maar om die te veranderen door pleitbezorging, activisme, intellectuele nauwgezetheid en een compromisloze zoektocht naar gerechtigheid. Hun kernstelling is uiteindelijk dat joodse bevrijding moet voortbestaan naast de bevrijding en waardigheid van andere volkeren.

Spinozism & Spinozist JudaismSpinozisme & spinozistisch jodendom

⚜ ⚜ ⚜

Baruch Spinoza (1632–1677) was born in Amsterdam to a Portuguese-Jewish family; Baruch is his given name, Benedictus in Latin. His Ethics argues that God and Nature are one substance (Deus sive Natura), that everything follows by necessity, and that freedom lies in understanding our place in that order. Spinozist Judaism is still forming, for strategic reasons, a secretive practice sustained by ethical doubt and skepticism of religious myths.

Dama di Louzada philosophizes; "Perhaps, creation and existence–fundamentally is a selfish and evil process, in which the destruction of living things is required for another to sustain itself, yet it does not make that existence inherently good, or evil."

"We see animals as animals, not good nor evil, though they do make choices, we argue they provide for their own nature, they lack the rationale and intelligence–means and circumstance to do otherwise, but this raises questions concerning humanity, the law recognises this in its provisions for mental illness in criminal conduct, so as to say, some individuals may be born–evil–predisposed to such actions, and commit such acts because of their nature,"

"Yet, I believe that one's inherent nature, be it man or beast, is devoid of ethics, and that is what G-d, or the universe, has created mankind to adjudicate upon and discover, a manner in which an ethical life is lived."

In a sense, living ethically in my view is the pathway to living a truly righteous life, inspite of adversity or temptation to do evil, in which many encouragements exist, and as we regard ourselves as ethical enough to judge our fellow man, we must be ethical enough to protect nature–the universe itself—from our nature and that of others."

"As a species with a great potential to do profound evil onto eachother, the world, ourselves, and our descendants—and a great potential to do the exact contrary, many humans have done both, and none of us are above human error in that regard."

"The first Spinozist tenant therefore presents itself, aspire toward a more ethical visage of one's present and future self, no laws, religion, nor government is above ethical scrutiny, nor is oneself, as uncomfortable as it may be."

The Baruch Lousadas moved in the same world: the Barbados–Amsterdam–Livorno Sephardic trading network. A 1694 Amsterdam deed shows David Baruch Louzada buying half a house on Swanenburgherstraat that bore a plaque reading "Isle of Barbados." It is also suggested by Julian Land that the Spinozas were likely related to the Lousadas, though they were certainly in-laws.

Within Kasa Baruh the reference is pointed: its rationalism is a baseline of La Santa Jenchi, and its loyalty chart refuses those who "maintain Spinoza's Cherem," the 1656 ban that expelled him from the Amsterdam community.

Spinozism leans closely toward Reformist, Humanist, and Progressive Judaism as suitable means for institutional refuge and coalition building, given their acceptance of individualised philosophical interpretations of Jewish Identity and Law. Kasa Baruh maintains that Humanist Judaism must extend into the Caribbean, Israel and Latin America, while the Dama believes Reformist Judaism must "Become more Radical and unconstrained by Orthodox Hegemonies."

Baruch Spinoza (1632–1677) werd in Amsterdam geboren in een Portugees-joodse familie; Baruch is zijn voornaam, Benedictus in het Latijn. In zijn Ethica betoogt hij dat God en Natuur één substantie zijn (Deus sive Natura), dat alles met noodzaak voortvloeit en dat vrijheid ligt in het begrijpen van onze plaats in die orde. Het spinozistisch jodendom is nog in wording, om strategische redenen een geheime praktijk, gedragen door ethische twijfel en scepsis tegenover religieuze mythen.

Dama di Louzada filosofeert: "Misschien is schepping en bestaan fundamenteel een egoïstisch en kwaadaardig proces, waarin de vernietiging van levende wezens nodig is opdat een ander zich in stand kan houden, maar dat maakt dat bestaan niet van nature goed of kwaad."

"We zien dieren als dieren, niet goed of kwaad; ze maken wel keuzes, maar we stellen dat ze voorzien in hun eigen natuur, zonder de rationaliteit, intelligentie, middelen en omstandigheden om anders te handelen. Dat roept vragen op over de mens; de wet erkent dit in haar bepalingen over geestesziekte bij strafbare feiten, alsof sommige individuen als kwaadaardig geboren kunnen worden, vatbaar voor zulke handelingen, en ze plegen vanwege hun natuur."

"Toch geloof ik dat de aangeboren natuur van mens of dier zonder ethiek is, en dat G-d, of het universum, de mensheid heeft geschapen om daarover te oordelen en te ontdekken hoe een ethisch leven te leiden."

"In zekere zin is ethisch leven in mijn ogen de weg naar een werkelijk rechtschapen leven, ondanks tegenspoed of de verleiding om kwaad te doen, waartoe veel aanmoedigingen bestaan. En omdat we onszelf ethisch genoeg achten om over onze medemens te oordelen, moeten we ethisch genoeg zijn om de natuur, het universum zelf, te beschermen tegen onze natuur en die van anderen."

"Als soort met groot potentieel om elkaar, de wereld, onszelf en onze nakomelingen diep kwaad te doen, en groot potentieel om precies het tegenovergestelde te doen, hebben veel mensen beide gedaan, en niemand van ons is verheven boven menselijke fouten."

"Het eerste spinozistische beginsel luidt dus: streef naar een ethischer gelaat van je huidige en toekomstige zelf; geen wet, religie of regering staat boven ethische toetsing, en jijzelf ook niet, hoe ongemakkelijk dat ook is."

De Baruch Lousada's bewogen zich in dezelfde wereld: het sefardische handelsnetwerk Barbados–Amsterdam–Livorno. Een Amsterdamse akte uit 1694 toont dat David Baruch Louzada de helft van een huis aan de Swanenburgherstraat kocht met een plaquette "Isle of Barbados". Julian Land suggereert ook dat de Spinoza's waarschijnlijk aan de Lousada's verwant waren, al waren ze zeker aangetrouwd.

Binnen Kasa Baruh is de verwijzing scherp: rationalisme is een uitgangspunt van La Santa Jenchi, en het loyaliteitsdiagram weigert wie "Spinoza's Cherem handhaven", de ban van 1656 die hem uit de Amsterdamse gemeenschap verstootte.

Het spinozisme leunt dicht tegen het hervormde, humanistische en progressieve jodendom aan als middel voor institutionele toevlucht en coalitievorming, gezien hun aanvaarding van geïndividualiseerde filosofische interpretaties van joodse identiteit en wet. Kasa Baruh vindt dat het humanistisch jodendom zich moet uitstrekken tot het Caribisch gebied, Israël en Latijns-Amerika, terwijl de Dama meent dat het hervormd jodendom "radicaler en onbelemmerd door orthodoxe hegemonieën" moet worden.
